# Quality pass — the twelve cards ranked by how the end product sounds

*2026-10-23 · senior audio-DSP engineer · read-only. Cost, sprint fit and delivery time ignored throughout. Inputs: `README.md` (the cards), `brainstorm/sound-truth.md` (built on, not repeated), `technical-synthesis.md`, `panel/other-players.md`, and the code under `Sources/AudioDSP/…` where a sound claim rests on it. Outside material opened this session: the ISO 226:2003 contour formula and coefficients (IoSR `iso226.m`), three AutoEq corrections for the WH-1000XM4 (oratory1990, crinacle, Rtings). Labels: a claim rests on code (`file:line`), on a report, on a named reference, or is marked **(from DSP principle)**; anything not checked is **unverified**. Nothing is called a founder decision unless a document says so.*

**Words used once.** *True peak* — the real peak of the waveform between samples; the ceiling is measured against it. *The net working* — the safety net turning the music down on more than the odd peak; a few peaks per minute is inaudible, sustained work is the "flat, no snap" sound. *Level-matched* — two things compared at the same loudness; the louder one otherwise wins by habit, not merit. *Coherent* — the same sound present in two channels at once, so they add up in level when folded. *Tilt* — a tone curve that raises one end of the spectrum relative to the other.

**Two refinements to the criteria (a music lover would judge this way).** Q1 should include *nothing softened*: the one artifact this chain can produce on ordinary stereo music is the net working on a loud master — not pumping, but drums losing their snap. Q6 must be level-matched *including every broadband offset the engine adds* (leveling, pre-gain, the compensation's own level change), or the test measures loudness, not quality. Q5 should add *the tone does not change when Spatial is switched on* — a room render that also changes the tonal balance is judged as EQ, not space.

## Part A — the cards

### Card 1 — Leveling
1. **A + C** (one fixed gain per track/album in Enhanced, the old leveller removed, Off/Auto/Album/Track, fixed cut for unanalysed tracks; *and* leveling in Pure as a cut on the device volume). Wins Q1 (the level never moves inside a song; the gain is capped by the track's true peak so the net sleeps), Q3 on every path, Q6 (a constant multiply is the most transparent processing there is). C's coarse device steps are below the ~1 dB level JND for music **(from DSP principle)** — see Part B-3.
2. **A alone** ≡ **D** in final sound (D is A later). Both lose Q3 on the daily driver: Pure on the XM4 has no leveling at all.
3. **C alone** (Pure only): Q3 on the daily driver only; nothing in Enhanced.
4. **B** (the old auto-leveller as "Adaptive"): loses Q1 and Q6 — up to 1 dB/s of level drift inside a song and the net squashing the chorus after a quiet intro (`LoudnessModule.cpp:276-299` per sound-truth; the mechanism is a slow compressor with the net as its gain control **(from DSP principle)**).
5. **E**: two levellers the day Intensity returns; the live guess moves the level mid-song. Loses Q1, Q2.
**Quality-best:** A with C as part of the same answer, not a follow-up. **Differs from the README?** In status, not pick: the README keeps A and calls C "a worthy follow-up". By sound, the daily driver without C fails Q3 outright — a 1980s master next to a modern one differs by 6–10 dB, which a listener notices without trying **(from DSP principle; typical integrated-loudness spread)**. One fact changes the picture: over Bluetooth the "bit-perfect" promise ends at the Mac's AAC encoder (sound-truth, Words), so Pure and Enhanced-with-nothing-on send the same bits into the same encoder; Enhanced + Leveling on the XM4 is therefore *the same sound* as Pure + C, with finer steps. What the interim (A without C) loses: all leveling where the founder listens most; noticed on every shuffle. Also see Part B-7: the target matters more than the README's "−14 matches Roon" suggests.

### Card 2 — Intensity and Clarity
1. **A ≡ B ≡ D** in sound (Intensity scales only Clarity depth and room amount as *settings*; B reaches the same sounds through more switches; D is A with Clarity audible a sprint earlier). The one thing that decides the sound is not the option but the rule: *never a crossfade*. Today's blend adds up to +3 dB at mid-knob (`DSPKernel.cpp:285-288` per sound-truth) — a level hump that fools every A/B (Q6) — and a room-rendered signal crossfaded with the dry one comb-filters against its own delays **(from DSP principle)** — a hollow tone (Q1).
2. **C** (a macro that moves the other sliders): the same sound when untouched; moving it changes level and undoes hand-set switches — Q2.
3. **E**: the hump stays; Clarity inaudible in S16.
**Quality-best:** A/B tie. **Differs?** No.

### Card 3 — Loudness compensation
*Level source:* 1. **A** (Master Gain + device volume where readable, one-time reference; manual slider on the soundbar) — the only one that follows the volume the listener actually turns (Q4). 2. **B** (manual slider everywhere) — a tone control; Q4 only if re-dialled each night; on the soundbar A ≡ B. 3. **C** (Master Gain only) — does nothing on the founder's gear; Q4 fails.
*Form:* at equal final loudness the three forms are one curve (sound-truth §a). By the net's behaviour: 1. **cut-the-mids with the "volume minus its own cut" rule** ≡ the appendix's hybrid (loudness-matched offset for the level reading, cut-the-mids for headroom) — the net never wakes, no loop. 2. **loudness-matched** — the net touches the loudest passages on the XM4's −1 dB ceiling (Q1 small). 3. **boost** — the net working constantly on loud masters (Q1 real).
**Two things the sound asks for that the README does not say.** (i) The curve is a *bass* tilt, not bass-and-treble: computed from ISO 226:2003 (IoSR `iso226.m`, opened), a 20 dB turn-down from 80 phon calls for +6.2 dB at 100 Hz, +8.2 at 50 Hz, +10.5 at 20 Hz — and +0.3 dB at 8 kHz, +1.4 at 10 kHz, slightly *negative* (−0.6 dB) at 2–4 kHz. US-TON-03's "+3–5 dB at 8–12 kHz" would add brightness the ear does not ask for — fizz (Q1). The +4 dB treble cap is never reached at 50 %. (ii) The level reading must include the leveling gain and any pre-gain (card 4): a loud master leveled down 8 dB is 8 dB quieter at the ear, and the compensation that ignores it is wrong by that much.
**Quality-best:** A, cut-the-mids with the level rule, bass-only curve, level = everything that changes the level at the ear. **Differs?** Same pick; differs in the curve (no treble lift) and the level definition. The one-evening prototype settles the *fraction and cap*, not the form — the form is settled by the net **(from DSP principle)**.

### Card 4 — Headroom
1. **A** (one ledger: per-band sum held at +12 dB, one pre-gain before the EQ, AutoEq's preamp replaced, the net's work shown) — the only option in which stacked boosts never put the net to sustained work (Q1), and the graph keeps drawing what plays (Q2).
2. **B** (separate budgets, sum displayed) — the net does the summing by squashing; the readout reports it afterwards. 3. **C** (+6 dB compensation budget) — taste + correction still unsummed. 4. **D** — nothing added up. B ≡ C ≡ D in the failure mode; they differ only in when it arrives.
**Quality-best:** A. **Differs?** Not in the option; in what the interim costs. The ledger has two numbers (sound-truth, Decision 4): a *loudness* offset (exists: −ΔL, `TrackLoudnessGain.cpp:104-120`) and a *peak-safe* pre-gain = ceiling − (track true peak + the curve's boost), which needs the true-peak field card 1 adds to the analysis anyway. Without it, a boosted EQ on a hot master over Bluetooth is today's sound: at −1 dBTP the net works > 1 dB up to 74 % of the time on a −9 LUFS master, EQ On up to 2.75 dB under Off (`OutputCeiling.h:6-8`). A listener with a bass preset on a loud familiar record hears that against Compare. See Part B-2 for why the pre-gain, not the ceiling, is the quality answer.

### Card 5 — The 5.1 / 7.1 fold
1. **A's final arrangement** (fold inside the engine before the one net, crossfeed after, per-track trim from the folded true peak): one net that rarely acts, crossfeed on surround, the EQ's level logic seeing the stereo you hear. Q1, Q3, Q5.
2. **A's interim** (matrix + trim in the spatial unit, two nets, no crossfeed on surround) ≡ **C with leveling On**: with the trim from the true max the second net never acts, so the only loss is crossfeed on surround. C + leveling ≡ trim + leveling in level, because leveling's own true-peak cap *is* the trim **(from DSP principle)**.
3. **C with leveling Off**: unsquashed but ~8 dB quieter than the stereo albums around it (Q3 real; Q1 none). Ranked above B because a reach for the volume beats an artifact.
4. **B as written** (final home, no trim): the net in the right place and working constantly on dense masters (Q1 real; up to +7.7 dB coherent sum, panel BS.775-4 Table 2).
5. **D**: squashed, crossfeed silent, "XF" shown.
**Quality-best:** A's final arrangement (= sound-truth's "A's place + B's trim"). **Differs?** No in substance. **What the interim loses:** crossfeed on surround albums — a crossfeed user hears their switch do nothing on a 5.1 album (noticed; the badge must say so); and leveling/−ΔL computed on the six-channel loudness, 1–2 LU above the folded figure (sound-truth, Decision 5) — borderline at the ~1 dB JND. The folded loudness can be computed in the same analysis pass as the folded true peak, which removes that loss inside the interim. The LFE question is Part B-1.

### Card 6 — EQ layers, Compare, per-headphone memory
1. **A** (one program of ordered layers, one read-back, Compare = "without *my* EQ" with correction and compensation left in, a separate "Bypass all") ≡ **C** (Compare with a menu: same sound, worse hand) ≡ **D** (S12 first: same sound, different order). Q2 (the graph is what plays), Q6 (a fair Compare).
2. **E** (S13 narrowed to a manual AutoEq import): *sound-identical to A for a correctly chosen file.* The three XM4 corrections in AutoEq (oratory1990, crinacle, Rtings — opened) agree within ~1 dB on every main feature, so the measurement source is not where it goes wrong; the model is (an XM5 file on an XM4 is a wrong correction, which sounds wrong — Q1). The full S13 buys *the right curve every time*, not a better curve.
3. **B** (three engines): small ripples where engines overlap and a graph that no longer matches (Q2 small, growing).
4. **F**: Compare means three things; AutoEq's −6.1 dB preamp stacks with −ΔL (corrected headphones 6 dB too quiet) or is dropped (bass-heavy tracks hit the −0.1 dB wired ceiling).
**Quality-best:** A. **Differs?** No. One addition for the sound: Compare's level match must include every broadband offset of the program — the ledger's pre-gain included — not only −ΔL of the taste layer (decision 28 matches loudness; `Realizer.mm:399-410` publishes the identity at 0 dB per sound-truth). Otherwise Compare is louder than EQ-on on hot masters and wins by habit.

### Card 7 — Correction and spatial switching themselves on
1. **A** (opt-in per device, offer-and-remember, a chip per stage) — the file on day one (Q2, the faithful constraint); never a double correction.
2. **C** (one global switch, then by device class) — the same sound once on; a misfire (a DAC read as "USB", a speaker DAC given a headphone curve) is a wrong correction, Q1 real when it happens.
3. **B** (auto-apply + Undo) — a processed first sound: on the XM4 the published correction is a −4.2 dB shelf at 105 Hz, −5.2 dB at 143 Hz, +6.1 dB at 2.3 kHz and a −6.1 dB preamp (oratory1990 AutoEq, opened) — a different headphone, 6 dB quieter, before anything was touched. Fails Q2; see Part B-6 for the sound-only reading.
4. **D**: processed first launch and a double correction on a second pair (twice the curve: −10 to −19 dB in the bass, +12 dB at 2.3 kHz — unmistakably wrong, Q1).
**Quality-best:** A, with the preview of Part B-6. **Differs?** No.

### Card 8 — Headphones: one control or three
1. **A** (one Off / Crossfeed / Spatial control, exclusive by construction; render at the output boundary for every width; room amount a parameter) ≡ **B** while B's greying holds; B's failure mode — two head-shadow filters stacked — is a smeared centre and a dull top (Q1 real).
2. **C** (render inside the engine, stereo only; the fold stays for surround): surround albums never get the room while stereo ones do (Q3/Q5 inconsistent), and "inside the blend" means a crossfade — the comb of card 2.
3. **D**: both run; the engine's own comment calls it a bug (`TargetState.h:100-105`).
**Quality-best:** A. **Differs?** No. Two sound rules to carry: correction stays *before* the render (the BRIR assumes a neutral headphone; the architecture already says correction is "for timbre only", `architecture.md:321`), and the BRIR set must be headphone-equalised so Spatial changes space, not tone — else the pre-S18 test is a timbre test **(from DSP principle)**.

### Card 9 — W2 and the Engine sprint's neighbours
**A ≡ B ≡ C ≡ D** in sound for the plumbing. Two sound items ride on this card and A is the only option that gives them an owner: the ceiling rule (−1 dB on any output whose rate differs from the engine's until the conversion after it is measured — Part B-2) and the below-44 kHz EQ gap (a Bluetooth headset with its microphone on plays the EQ flat while the screen shows it on — Q2). **Quality-best:** A. **Differs?** No. The "0.9 dB lower peaks on a non-48 kHz wired output until the pre-gain" is the net working slightly more on hot masters with boosts — small.

### Card 10 — The litmus test and the name clash
**A ≡ C** in the end product's sound (a losing stage ships off either way; A learns it before the sprint is spent). **B** (skip the test) ships the same off-by-default stages but with card 11's front door a losing stage can be switched on by one tap — Q6 at risk. **D** (rename the feature) — no sound difference. **Quality-best:** A. **Differs?** No. Three refinements for the test to measure quality rather than loudness or tone: match within 0.2 dB *including every broadband offset*; run Loudness at the founder's real late-night level with the reference calibrated; run Spatial against a timbre-matched dry reference (same correction, headphone-equalised BRIR) with the artifact checklist.

### Card 11 — An "Enhance" front door
**A ≡ B** in sound (the same winning stages; one tap or several). **C** (everything on) turns on stages that lost — Q6 fails by construction. **Quality-best:** A/B tie. **Differs?** No. One guard: Enhance must not switch on Loudness before its reference is calibrated — an uncalibrated loudness button is a tone control applied at the wrong level.

### Card 12 — What proceeds now
**A ≡ B ≡ C** — no sound difference; the player half touches no audio processing. **Quality-best:** tie. **Differs?** No.

## Part B — quality-only questions

**1. The fold's LFE channel.** What omitting it loses: on a music surround mix whose sub-bass lives *only* in the LFE, the bottom octave (roughly 20–80 Hz) — audible on the XM4 and on a soundbar with a sub (Q4, Q6 on those albums). On a mix whose LFE is a low-passed *copy* of the bass already in the mains — common film practice **(from practice; not opened)** — nothing is lost, and *including* it adds that bass twice: +6 dB below ~120 Hz at full level, +4.6 dB at 0.707, +3.5 dB at 0.5 — boomy (Q1). The LFE is also stored 10 dB below its intended playback level (the film convention; **unverified this session**), so even "full level" into both ears is ~7 dB under what the mixer heard (with ~3 dB of two-ear summation, **from DSP principle**). No single level is right for both kinds of album; that is why BS.775-4 says it is "often not included" (panel quote) and why the shipped decision omits it (FD-9; US-R1F-08 "LFE omitted"). The quality answer is **per track, from the analysis** the fold already needs: if the LFE is coherent with the mains' low band, omit (redundant); if it carries energy the mains do not, include at 0 dB per side (the conservative end; still under the mixer's level), and let the per-track trim — computed from the folded true peak *with* that LFE gain — keep the net asleep. Default when unknown: omit. **What to listen to:** run the folded analysis on the founder's 5.1 albums and pick one where the LFE has bass the mains do not, and one where it is a copy; on the XM4, a bass-heavy passage three ways (omit / −6 dB / 0 dB) with the trim applied. The loudness measurement already ignores LFE (`ChannelLayout.h:11-13`, BS.1770 weight 0), so leveling is unaffected either way.

**2. The ceiling.** The sound facts: at −1 dBTP with the EQ on, the net works > 1 dB up to 74 % of the time on a −9 LUFS master (`OutputCeiling.h:6-8`); decision 29's −0.1 dBTP cut heavy limiting from 26 % to 7 % of the time, On/Off mismatch p90 0.57 dB (`s10-8-glass-sweep-plan.md:118-121`). The limiter is a 3 ms look-ahead true-peak design with a −0.27 dB working margin (`LimiterModule.h:11-37, 103-105`). **Which sounds better:** on a lossless local output at the engine's rate (the HDMI soundbar; a 48 kHz USB DAC), −0.1 dBTP — fewer softened transients, and no more exposure than the untouched file already sends through the same DAC. On Bluetooth, −1 dBTP — the AAC encoder overshoots on decode (EBU R128's rationale, panel). On a wired output at *another* rate, −1 dBTP until the conversion after the engine is measured — the exposure equals a bit-perfect Off's, but it is unmeasured. **Does −ΔL already solve "EQ On sounds quieter"?** Only half of it. Decision 26's −ΔL makes EQ On the same *loudness* as Off; decision 29 was about the *net* taking up to 2.75 dB off the peaks — a peak problem −ΔL cannot touch. The honest physics: a hot master with +6 dB of bass has nowhere to go; you either limit it (softened) or turn it down (quieter). Ranked by sound: (1) turn it down by exactly the peak-safe pre-gain, show it, and level-match Compare to it — the net never acts at any ceiling, which makes the moving ceiling moot; (2) −0.1 dBTP (limit as little as possible); (3) −1 dBTP with EQ on. "Quieter by the pre-gain" is the price of a hot master, and a constant offset is far more transparent than sustained limiting **(from DSP principle)**. So: keep decision 29 where the link is lossless *and* the rate matches; −1 dBTP elsewhere; solve the quiet-vs-squash trade at the root with the pre-gain. The pre-gain is exact only if the analysis measures the EQ'd track's true peak rather than bounding it by the curve's maximum boost (the "per-curve peak scan" listed as later in the EQ engine notes) — that precision is worth having: a worst-case bound turns down more than needed on most tracks.

**3. Leveling in Pure via the device-volume loan (card 1 C).** A sound-quality gain for the daily driver, with one precondition. Gain: even levels where today there are none (Q3). Risks: (i) coarse steps — Bluetooth absolute volume is a 0–127 scale that hosts divide into steps (forum-level; **unverified**); the XM4's own curve is unknown, so a −4.2 dB cut may land at −3.5 or −4.8. The level JND for music is about 1 dB **(from DSP principle)**; a listener does not notice half a step between tracks, and never inside a track (the cut is set at the seam). (ii) compounding — the loan reads any device reading that differs from its last set as the listener's and re-anchors (`MasterGainOnPure.swift:312-329`; `sameLevel` 0.05 dB at `:179`); an untaught loan would creep down track after track — audible and wrong (Q2). That is a correctness precondition, not a sound trade. (iii) cut-only: quiet albums cannot be raised; at a −18 target that is almost nothing (Part B-7). Sound equivalence worth stating: on Bluetooth, Pure + C and Enhanced + Leveling are the same bits into the same encoder; on a USB DAC, a cut on the DAC's own volume and a 32-bit float cut in the app are both inaudible at any sane level **(from DSP principle)**. So C's sound value is "leveling on Pure", not "better leveling".

**4. Loudness-compensation depth.** From ISO 226:2003 (coefficients and formula opened via IoSR `iso226.m`; the printed standard not opened): the full contour difference for a 20 dB turn-down (80 → 60 phon) is +6.2 dB at 100 Hz, +8.2 at 50 Hz, +10.5 at 20 Hz, +3.9 at 200 Hz, +1.2 at 500 Hz, ~0 from 1 to 8 kHz, +1.4 at 10 kHz; for a 10 dB turn-down, about half. FR-TONAL-03's 50 % (candidate 30–75 %, "precedent 40–60 %") is a stated provisional, not a measured optimum; I know of no published listening test that settles the fraction, and none was opened. The physical reasons the right number is well under 100 % **(from DSP principle)**: the contours are pure-tone, in-quiet data, and for broadband music the masking between neighbouring bands reduces how much the balance shifts with level (the Moore–Glasberg partial-loudness view); the mix engineer's monitoring level is unknown, so the "reference" is a guess (Holman & Kampmann 1978, JAES 26(7/8), argued exactly this — only the abstract was reachable); and a quieter listen is *expected* to sound a little leaner. At 50 % and a 20 dB turn-down: +3 dB at 100 Hz, +4 at 50 Hz, +5 at 20 Hz, nothing above 1 kHz — the +6 dB bass cap engages only at full fraction and deep bass; the treble cap never. **What too much sounds like:** thick, boomy bass that blurs kick and bass-guitar notes; vocals pushed back (in the cut-the-mids form that is literally the mids going down); and the tone chasing the volume knob — every touch of the volume changes the record (Q2). What too little sounds like: nothing — the ordinary thin late-night sound. Start at 50 %, bass-only, and let the prototype evening move it between 40 and 60.

**5. Headphone correction: full identification and library vs manual import.** For a correctly chosen file the two are **identical in sound** — the same filters play. The three XM4 corrections in AutoEq (oratory1990 on GRAS 43AG; crinacle on GRAS 43AG-7; Rtings on B&K 5128) agree within ~1 dB on their main features, so the library's choice of source barely matters on this model. What the full version buys in sound is the avoidance of the *wrong* curve: the wrong model (XM4 vs XM5), the ANC-on vs ANC-off variant (AutoEq lists both for the XM4, from HypetheSonics), or a stale file — each of which is a correction that sounds wrong (Q1). Identification cannot know the ANC state or the codec either, so the "right curve" is still a guess within ~1–2 dB **(from DSP principle)**. A listener on one pair who imports the right file loses nothing.

**6. Auto-apply vs offer, judged only by the first sound.** By the first sound alone, a Harman-target correction is what most listeners prefer in level-matched tests (Olive, Welti & McMullin 2013, AES paper 8867: the most preferred of six targets; the exact majority split was not retrievable — from memory about two-thirds, with sizeable "more bass" and "less bass" minorities — **unverified**). So on average, B's first sound wins Q4/Q6 — *if* level-matched. It is not: the file carries a −6.1 dB preamp, so the sighted first impression is "quieter and thinner", which loses by habit; and the XM4's bass is part of why people choose it, so the minority is not small. And it fails Q2 and the faithful constraint on every new output. This does not change the faithful-default answer; it argues for the **third way**: the offer carries a hold-to-hear preview — Compare in reverse, *with* the correction, level-matched by the ledger (the preamp replaced by the loudness match) — so the first sound is the file, the second is the corrected headphone at equal loudness, and the listener's own ear decides. In sound that is A's day one plus B's advantage, without B's surprise.

**7. Leveling target −14 / −16 / −18.** No tonal difference and no artifact at any of them — the true-peak cap keeps the net asleep. Three real differences. (i) *Consistency (Q3):* the cap means a track can only be raised until its peak meets the ceiling. Dynamic masters (classical, jazz, much pre-1990 rock: −18 to −22 LUFS with peaks near full scale) cannot reach −14 and sit 2–6 dB under it while loud masters sit exactly on it — the leveling is *less* even at −14 than at −18, where almost everything is a cut and reaches the target. This is why ReplayGain/foobar2000 use −18 and EBU −23 (panel). (ii) *Where the knob sits:* 4 dB higher at −18 — no cost on the XM4 (its own amplifier) or a DAC with its own volume; on the Mac's digital volume, 4 dB of 24-bit headroom — inaudible **(from DSP principle)**. (iii) *The compensation's reference* must be calibrated with leveling at the chosen target (or read the leveling gain), or changing the target later changes the compensation. Quality-best: −18 (even −20 is defensible); "−14 matches Roon and Spotify" is a familiarity reason, not a sound one. Today's −16 (`AudioConstants.h:34`) is the compromise.

**8. Where quality-first asks for more than the recommendation.** (a) *Own analysis over tags* (card 1): our BS.1770 integrated loudness with true peak is more trustworthy than a tag from an unknown scanner (ReplayGain 1.0 and R128 scanners disagree by 1–3 dB on the same file **(from practice; unverified to the decibel)**); read tags only for a track not yet analysed, ahead of the fixed −5 dB cut. (b) *The folded loudness and the folded true peak in one analysis pass* (card 5), so surround albums level exactly even in the interim. (c) *A per-track LFE decision from coherence* (B-1). (d) *The peak-safe pre-gain measured, not bounded* (B-2), with Compare level-matched to it (card 6). (e) *The compensation realised as the ISO 226 difference curve itself* — smooth, per level, bass-only — through the one EQ program, with the level reading including leveling and pre-gain (card 3). (f) *The fold's trim from the true maximum* when leveling is On (leveling fixes consistency, so the backstop never acts); a high percentile only when leveling is Off. (g) *Correction before the render, headphone-equalised BRIRs, head tracking opt-in* (card 8; `architecture.md:321`) — externalisation (Q5) without a tone change. (h) *The litmus ladder matched over every broadband offset* (card 10). Each of these buys a specific sound: even levels on every album, a net that never works, a loudness button that does not fizz, a Compare and a blind test that measure quality rather than loudness.

## Part C — where ranking by sound changes the answer most

**One — leveling must reach the daily driver, at a target every album can reach:** over Bluetooth, Pure and bare Enhanced are the same bits into the same encoder, so card 1's option C is part of the answer rather than a follow-up, and −18 beats −14 because the true-peak cap lets every album reach −18 and only the loud ones reach −14.

**Two — headroom by turning down, never by limiting:** the peak-safe pre-gain (a one-number consequence of the true-peak field card 1 adds) replaces "limit a little less" (decision 29's moving ceiling) with "never limit", which also makes card 5's trim the same rule and card 4's ledger a refinement rather than the fix.

**Three — two fixed rules become per-track decisions:** the fold should decide LFE from the analysis (omit when it is a copy of the mains, include when it is the only bass), and the correction offer should carry a level-matched hold-to-hear preview, so the correction's real sound advantage is heard on day one without breaking the faithful default.
